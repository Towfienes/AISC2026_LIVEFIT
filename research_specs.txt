TOPIC: variance-reduction
RECOMMENDATION: Post-stratified CUPAC: a session-stratified studentized statistic plus a single frozen, exogenous prognostic score g(X) applied as a CUPED pseudo-outcome inside the existing Fisher randomization test (Zhao & Ding 2021 pseudo-outcome strategy). Two layers, one estimator, one new scalar covariate. Explicitly NOT DML/DR, NOT MLRATE's t-test inference layer, NOT the pre-block covariates the codebase already computes.
EXPECTED GAIN: Layer 1 (session stratification) is the larger and safer half. Analytically, leakage variance divided by within variance is about 0.65 x (sigma_between/sigma_within)^2 for n_s=16 blocks per session: at ratio 0.5 that is a 14% variance cut, at 1.0 a 39% cut, at 1.5 a 59% cut. Live commerce across different hosts, products and days plausibly gives ratio >= 1, so budget 15-40% variance reduction, free of modelling assumptions. Layer 2 (CUPAC on exogenous features only) should be forecast at out-of-fold R^2 = 0.10-0.25 against the within-session-demeaned outcome, NOT the 40-73% quoted by Deng et al. or Pankratev & Arora — those require a per-unit pre-period LiveLift does not have. Combined variance factor about 0.75 x 0.82 = 0.62, so MDE x sqrt(0.62) = 0.78. Reported MDE 20% becomes roughly 15-18%, with 16% as the honest central forecast. Equivalently about 35-40% fewer sessions for the same MDE — the 18-session scenario buys what 28 sessions buy today. Two hard caveats: this does NOT reach 10%, and if the Gate-0 Poisson floor shows reducible_share < 0.15 then layer 2 delivers essentially nothing and only layer 1's 15-40% is real.
WHY IT FITS: Four reasons. (1) Cluster count: at 18-30 sessions LiveLift is below every threshold in the literature; Pankratev 2026 names CUPAC as the method that still works at ncl in [20,100] and names DML-DR as the one that breaks there. (2) Degrees of freedom: Lei & Ding (Biometrika 108(4), 2021) require p small relative to n for Lin's estimator to stay consistent and asymptotically normal, and for cluster-robust inference the effective n is the CLUSTER count, not the 300-450 blocks. Three to five raw covariates plus Lin interactions cost 6-10 parameters against 18-30 clusters. Collapsing them into ONE cross-fitted score costs 1-2 regardless of how many features feed the model — this is precisely why MLRATE and CUPAC enter the ML prediction as a single regressor. (3) The randomization test makes over-fitting non-fatal: Zhao & Ding prove finite-sample exactness under the sharp null for any adjustm

TOPIC: anytime-valid
RECOMMENDATION: Hierarchical Bayesian Poisson rate model (HBP) with partial pooling — clicks ~ Poisson, log link, product and session random intercepts, log-exposure as a free "quasi-offset", ~8 fixed effects — fitted by Laplace approximation via `statsmodels.genmod.bayes_mixed_glm.PoissonBayesMixedGLM.fit_map`, emitting a posterior credible interval on the MEAN click rate (clicks per 1000 viewer-seconds — the same unit as the pre-registered primary outcome), with the interval half-width scaled by a single global factor kappa calibrated by leave-one-session-out replay against a top-arm-retention target of 90%.
EXPECTED GAIN: Three gains, in descending order of how much they matter. (1) The largest is POWER, indirectly. At 18-30 clusters and 300-450 blocks the team cannot buy more n — the MDE of ~20% is fixed by the design. The only remaining lever is making the treatment stronger, and the treatment IS the product brain. With a plausible across-SKU spread in true click rate of ~25% (CV) and a ranker correlating ~0.5 with truth, choosing the argmax rather than uniformly among 3 candidates lifts the ON-arm rate by roughly 6-12%. Moving a true ATE from, say, 15% to 22-25% is the difference between a study that reports a null and one that clears its own MDE. (2) Estimation quality: partial pooling at ~15-25 observations per SKU typically cuts per-product rate MSE by 25-40% versus unpooled empirical means, and unlike the current heuristic it produces a number in the outcome's own units, so the card's `estimate` field finally has a source. (3) Inner-tier validity: today the logged `inner_propensity` is derived from a half-width on a different scale than the score, so it is not interpretable and no off-policy analysis of the inner tier is defensible. After this change the propensities are meaningful and the OPE in Step 7 becomes possible at all. Expect the mean overlap-set size to fall from 3 in the first two or three sessions to 1.5-2 by session 15 — report that curve, it is the single most legible piece of evidence that the brain is learning.
WHY IT FITS: Five reasons, each tied to a specific constraint of this design. (1) Right quantity for the elimination rule: `choose_action` compares intervals to decide whether the model can distinguish two products. That is a question about the mean, and a Bayesian posterior on the linear predictor answers exactly it; the interval narrows as evidence accrues, so k naturally walks 3 -> 2 -> 1 over the session series, which is the "controlled exploration" contract the docstring already promises but the current `0.5/sqrt(1+n_clicks)` fabricates. (2) Right unit: predicting clicks per 1000 viewer-seconds makes the product brain forecast the very quantity the switchback measures. The card's `estimate` field becomes a real number with a source, satisfying the hard constraint, and the interval stays internal in `candidates_json` where the schema already forbids it from surfacing.
